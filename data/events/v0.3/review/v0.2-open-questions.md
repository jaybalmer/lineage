# Open questions from the v0.2 podium wave

## baker-grassroots

- DIVISION VOCABULARY NEEDS EXTENDING. The brief's `division` list (mens, womens, open, masters, juniors, groms, pro, am) cannot express the Baker Honor Roll's actual divisions, which are a two-axis grid of ability/age class x gender: e.g. 'Pro Women Masters Ages 40+', 'Mid Masters Men Ages 40-49', 'Next Gen Girls Ages 12 & Under'. Collapsing these to a single token would flatten distinct divisions into one, so every result row here carries an extra `division_label` field holding the source's exact division wording, with `division` as the nearest vocabulary match. Recommend adopting `division_label` (or splitting division into class + gender) before load.
- Divisions with no vocabulary equivalent at all, recorded as division 'open' with the source wording in `division_label`: 'NON-BINARY (all ages)' (Baker 2025), 'PARA SNOWBOARD (all ages)' (Baker 2025), 'SWITCH MEN / SWITCH WOMEN (all ages)' (Baker 2025), 'FASTEST TIME OVERALL' (Baker 2025, an award with a single placing, not a three-deep division), 'Sit-Ski' (Dirksen Derby), 'Men's/Women's Splitboard' (Dirksen Derby). The Baker Honor Roll's 'Open All Ages' column corresponds to the 2025 finals PDF's NON-BINARY category.
- SOURCE CONFLICT, Baker 2025 Grand Masters. The Honor Roll's 2025 row puts Jen Jones under 'Grand Masters Men Ages 50+' and Tim Gallagher under 'Grand Masters Women Ages 50+'. The official 2025 finals PDF has it the other way round: GRAND MASTERS WOMEN 1st Jen Jones (1:26.41), GRAND MASTERS MEN 1st Tim Gallagher (1:12.43). The two cells are transposed on the Honor Roll. The PDF is used here. The same two columns are correct on the Honor Roll for 2023 (checked against the 2023 finals PDF), so this is a one-year data-entry error rather than a systematic column problem - but it is worth reporting to Mt Baker.
- DATA ANOMALY, Baker 'Grand Masters Women Ages 50+' column, 2007-2016. That column holds apparently-male names in six years: Larry Freeman (2007), Bob Satushek (2008, 2009, 2010, 2011) and Kevin Boyce (2016), while the adjacent 'Grand Masters Men' column holds different names in the same rows. These rows are recorded as printed but marked confidence 'likely', because the division attribution is probably wrong on the source - most plausibly the column was used for a second men's age band before 'Super Masters 60+' was introduced. Needs a human to check the live page or ask the organisers.
- NAME VARIANTS across Baker sources: 'Barrett Christy' (Honor Roll) and 'Barrett Cummins' (2017 Snowboard Magazine list, 2023 and 2025 finals PDFs) are the same rider; 'Pontus Stahlkloo' (Honor Roll), 'Pontus Staahlkloo' (2023 PDF) and 'Pontus Stahlkloo' with ring-a (2025 PDF) likewise; 'Kiana Putnam' (2023 PDF) vs 'Kiana Putman' (2025 PDF). Recorded as printed by each source; needs rider-identity reconciliation at load time.
- WHAT THE BAKER HONOR ROLL DOES NOT GIVE: it is a winners-only table - one name per division per year, no 2nd or 3rd place, no finish times, no nationalities, and no hometowns or ages. Full three-deep podiums with times exist only for 2023 and 2025 (organiser finals PDFs) and 2017 (Snowboard Magazine). Times for 1985-2016, 2018-2020 were not found anywhere. The old results system at lbs.mtbaker.us/lbs/index.php/results now redirects to the current-year page, and no per-year results PDFs older than 2023 were found on the site.
- Mt Baker editions left with no results, 7 of the 43 in the input: 2005 and 2015 (cancelled - the Honor Roll simply has no row for either), 2021, 2022 and 2024 (no Honor Roll row; the input file marks 2021 and 2022 cancelled and 2024 postponed), 2026 (postponed) and 2027 (not yet held). Separately, 1991 is covered only in the Pro table (Craig Kelly, Jean Higgins): the Amateur table prints a 1991 row with every cell empty, so no amateur winner is recorded for that year.
- DIRKSEN DERBY is the biggest gap. The organiser keeps no public per-year results archive: the per-edition history pages (dirksenderby.com/Race/DirksenDerby/Page-8 through Page-28) carry only date, venue, racer count and poster artist, with no placings. The RunSignup results hub at dirksenderby.com/Race/Results/135169 does list named result sets per year ('DD17 Final Results', 'DD16 Splitboard Results', years 2007-2025) but renders them via JavaScript and returned 'Please check back later for results' to a plain fetch. Retrieving those result sets - most likely via the RunSignup results API with the right resultSetId - would fill 15 of the 18 Dirksen editions in one pass and is the single highest-value follow-up in this domain.
- Dirksen Derby editions filled here: 2014 (8th, full podiums with times), 2015 (9th, top three in five categories, no times) and 2017-b / Dirksen Derby 10 (top three in three categories, no times). Nothing found for 2007-2013, 2016, 2018-2020, 2022-2025.
- Dirksen Derby 14 (2021) deliberately left empty. The Snowboard Magazine recap names Nils Mindnich and Nelly Steinhoff as 'taking top honors' in a photo caption without naming a division, and refers to Devon Raney (adaptive), Dev Gupta (non-binary) and Jake Price winning 'his category' without stating which. Under the brief's rule against reconstructing a podium from a caption, none of these were recorded.
- Dirksen Derby 2012 (6th annual): the ESPN recap at espn.com/action/snowboarding/story/_/id/8756651 returned empty content on repeated fetches. Its headline states that Austin Smith, Maria DeBari, Gerry Lopez and Ralph Backstrom 'win' the sixth annual Derby, but it does not say which category each won, so nothing was recorded. Worth retrying via a Wayback capture, which was not reachable from this environment.
- PACWEST BANKED SLALOM: only the two 2022 open champions were recoverable (Kevin Nolan, Marni Yamada), from the Snowboarder's Journal recap. The results PDF that recap links to, summitatsnoqualmie.com/documents/Summit/events/pacwest-results-2022.pdf, now returns 404, and the current Summit event page links no results for any year. The 2024 Snowboarder's Journal piece is a narrative recap containing no placings at all. PacWest has since moved registration to LiveHeats (liveheats.com/events/464357), which may hold 2023-2025 results.
- HOLY OLY REVIVAL: the 2010 (7th annual) Snowboarder recap is mostly a list of named awards rather than placings - Northwest Method Award, Billy Goat's Gruff, Mayor of Tube City, Berserker Award and so on - which are not podium places and were not recorded. Only 'Holiest of the Oly' gave a ranked 1st and 2nd (Austin Hironaka, Nate Farrell); no 3rd was stated. Jess Kimura is listed against a women's category with no place given, so no placing was recorded for her. Discipline is set to 'other' for these rows because 'Holiest of the Oly' is an overall award spanning the event's quarterpipe and jib features and no source assigns it to one. For 2007 (4th annual) the Lib Tech blog post is no longer retrievable at its indexed URL - the blog index returns current posts instead - so no 2007 results were found.
- USASA NATIONAL CHAMPIONSHIPS: still no usable public archive, confirming the wave 1 finding. usasa.org does publish per-event pages (e.g. usasa.org/national-championships/events/sb-group-5-halfpipe-341993/) but they returned 403 to this environment, and USASA nationals award podiums across roughly 40 age-class groups per discipline, so even with access this is a several-hundred-row mining task in its own right rather than something to fold into this domain. No results recorded for the 1990, 2023, 2025 or 2026 editions.
- HOT DAWGZ & HAND RAILS: no results found for the 2012 (9th annual) edition in the input file - contemporaneous coverage (First Tracks, KBHR) is preview-only. Results were found for adjacent editions not in the input: 2013 (10th, added to new_editions) and, unopened but indexed, 2015 (Zak Hale) and further years won by Jaeger Bailey and Melissa Evans at snowboarder.com. Wave 1's edition list for this series is incomplete.

## canada

- Snoboard Shop North American Snowboarding Championships, 1985-1988: NO podium list for any year was found online. Searched: Kootenay Mountain Culture / Mountain Culture Group 'Chairmen of the Board' feature, Banff Norquay's own 'Snowboarding Arrives' history post, SnowboardQuebec's 'histoire' page, Whistler Museum's Ken Achenbach and Snoboard Shop tag pages, snowboarder.com's TransWorld archive (including the Fall 1987 and Sept/Oct 1988 issue pages), Barfoot Snowboards' own timeline, and the TransWorld 1980s history timeline. Direct access to archive.org and newspapers.com is blocked from this environment, so Calgary Herald / Alberta newspaper microfilm was NOT searched. QUESTION FOR THE COMMUNITY: who won the Snoboard Shop North American Snowboarding Championships in 1985, 1986, 1987 and 1988, and in which categories (the event is described as having run halfpipe/moguls/racing but no source names the format)?
- Snoboard Shop championships, best lead found: TransWorld SNOWboarding's September/October 1988 issue (vol 2 no 1, the 'Special Contest Issue') carries a contest report titled 'SnowBoard Shop and Barfoot Snowboards North American Snowboard Championships (Banff, Canada)'. The snowboarder.com archive page for that issue lists the contents but not the results. A scan or a physical copy of TWS 2.1 (Sept/Oct 1988) should contain the full 1988 podium. Likewise TWS Fall 1987 (premiere issue) carries a feature called 'Miles Apart' comparing the Breckenridge and Banff contests, which likely names the 1987 placings. ASK: does anyone have TWS 2.1 or the 1987 premiere issue?
- Snoboard Shop championships 1985 and 1986: no venue confirmed. Wave 1 sourced 1987 to Sunshine Village and 1988 to Mt Norquay (Norquay's own history says Sunshine 'abandoned' the event mid-season in 1988 and Norquay took it on). SnowboardQuebec says the series began in 1985. The only 1985 result found (Jose Fernandes, 3rd) is described as being 'in Calgary'. ASK: was the 1985 and 1986 event at Sunshine Village, at Paskapoo/Canada Olympic Park in Calgary, or somewhere else?
- Snoboard Shop 1985 podium: snowboarding8090.com states Swiss rider Jose Fernandes 'got third in the North American Championships in Calgary' in 1985, and Fernandes himself confirms in a 2023 Radical Sports interview that he flew to 'America/Canada' for a competition for the first time in 1985 and that Tom Sims, Terry Kidwell, Mark Heingartner, Andy Coghlan and Bob Klein were there. Recorded here as place 3, confidence likely. NOT confirmed: who finished 1st and 2nd, and the discipline. Jose Fernandes is alive and gives interviews - he is the single best living source for the 1985 result.
- Achenbach oral history: Ken Achenbach has posted 'old life story' videos about founding the Snoboard Shop on his own Facebook page and is active in Calgary. He is the most direct route to the 1985-88 results, entry lists and prize breakdown, and possibly to physical result sheets or posters. Recommend a direct approach rather than more web searching.
- Canada Snowboard nationals: FIS turns out to be a productive archive for the pre-2015 years that canadasnowboard.ca does not cover. Use https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/general/calendar-results.html?sectorcode=SB&seasoncode=YYYY&categorycode=NC&nationcode=CAN . Confirmed NC events found and recorded as new editions: 2001 Big White (event id 7071), 2002 Tremblant (7859), 2003 Calgary/Nakiska (11010), 2004 Quebec (11816), 2005 Big White (15436), 2014 Mt Tremblant (34397), 2018 Calgary (42569). Seasons 2006-2013, 2015-2017 and 2019-2026 were NOT swept for NC events for lack of budget - that sweep is cheap and would likely yield a near-complete alpine/SBX/halfpipe national champion list back to 2001.
- Canada Snowboard nationals, podiums not harvested (FIS race ids known, just needs fetching): 2001 giant slalom men (raceid 1779) and women (1780); 2002 PGS men (2045), PGS women (2044), GS men (2048), GS women (2049); 2003 PGS men (3225), PGS women (3224), PSL men (3227), PSL women (3226); 2005 SBX men (4716), SBX women (4717), halfpipe women (4939); 2004 and 2014 all races. - 2026 Speed Nation SBX National Championship (Mont Sainte-Anne, 31 March 2026): no results published yet at the time of research. - 2026 Air Nation Halfpipe National Championship (WinSport COP, 20 Feb 2026) is marked cancelled in the wave 1 data. Not re-checked. Worth confirming whether a halfpipe national title was awarded in 2026 at all.
- Para snowboard national titles: Canada Snowboard publishes them (e.g. 2018 Big White: Curt Minard men's upper limb, Alex 'Bubz' Massie men's lower limb, Michelle Salt women's lower limb; 2017 Tremblant: Curt Minard, Sandrine Hamel, Gerrit Van Bruggen, John Leslie, Alex Massie, Andrew Genge, Jakob Bouchard). - SCHEMA GAP that is costing this domain real data: `division` is a single field mixing gender (mens/womens) with class (juniors/masters/pro/am). It cannot express 'junior women', 'U15 men', 'women's pro' or a para class. Because of this, roughly 60 sourced Canadian podium placings were left out of this file: all junior and U15 national titles for 2017, 2018 and 2019, all para national titles, and the whole age-group ladder of the Neil Edgeworth Memorial Banked Slalom (about 22 age categories per year, from 'Female 11 and under' to 'Male 60 and over'). Recommend splitting division into `gender` + `class` before wave 3; the NEBS PDFs and the Canada Snowboard roundups can then be re-harvested cheaply from the URLs already cited here.

## fis-worlds

- FIS results pages for World Championship events before roughly 2013 print only the FIS points awarded for the placing (1000/800/600), not a run score or a race time. score_or_time is therefore null for most 1996-2011 rows even though the podium itself is fully sourced.
- Parallel events (PGS/PSL) on FIS pages print each finalist's individual final-run time, which is not monotonic with the finishing rank (the loser of a final can show a faster raw time). I left score_or_time null on all parallel rows rather than record a value that looks like a contradiction. The times are on the linked race pages if the parent wants them.
- Several editions run two different team events (Parallel Team and Snowboard Cross Team) in the same year, which collide under discipline 'team' + division 'open'. I added an optional event_name field on those rows to disambiguate. 2017 Sierra Nevada is different again: its two team events were men's and women's SBX team, so those carry division mens/womens.
- FIS Junior Worlds 2021 (season 2021) is split across two venues that BOTH ran the full parallel programme: Lachtal AUT 19-21 Dec 2020 and Krasnoyarsk RUS 25-27 Mar 2021. That produces two PGS, two PSL and two Parallel Team podiums under one edition_id. I recorded both and used event_name to say which venue and date each belongs to. The parent may want to split this into two edition rows.
- 2019 Park City: the men's and women's Big Air were listed on the FIS event page as cancelled, so no big-air podium exists for that edition. The FIS pages also give the venue for the 2019 snowboard cross and team events as Solitude, not Park City.
- 2023 Junior Worlds halfpipe (Cardrona NZL, 8 Sep 2023, raceids 20879 and 20880) is on the FIS calendar but both pages return 'No results found for this competition'. No junior halfpipe podium recorded for 2023.
- 2026 Junior Worlds at Calgary included a 'Street Style' event (raceids 24330, 24331). There is no street-style term in the brief's discipline vocabulary; I mapped it to 'jib' and put the printed FIS name in event_name. Flagging in case the vocabulary should gain a 'street' or 'rail-jam' value instead.
- ISF world championships 2000 (Davos, Jakobshorn): snowboarden.de's contemporaneous report gives four podiums under the heading of the Motorola ISF World Pro Tour final - 'World Championships Alpine' men and women, and the halfpipe final men and women. The alpine race format is not named beyond 'Alpine', so I used discipline 'other' rather than guess giant-slalom. The same page also carries an 'Alpine Duel' podium which appears to be a separate competition at the same finals; I did not record it against this edition.
- ISF World Championships 1993 (Ischgl): no results source found. Searched FIS (which has no ISF data), the German Wikipedia Snowboard-Weltmeisterschaften article (mentions the 1993-1999 ISF championships existed but lists no medallists and cites no results source), and snowboarder.com's 1990s history timeline. The only hits were unsourced Usenet posts, which the brief excludes. No podium recorded.
- World Snowboarding Championships (Sims) 1983, 1984, 1985 at Soda Springs and 1986, 1987 at Breckenridge: read both snowboarder.com sources cited on those edition rows (the 1980s history timeline and 'A Complete History of the Snowboard Halfpipe'). Both describe the contests in detail - the pipe build, the Burton boycott threat, the Swatch money - but neither names a single winner or podium. No results recorded for any of the five editions, nor for the 1987 European World Snowboarding Championships at Livigno/St. Moritz.
- World Snowboarding Championships 2001 (Whistler): the Pique Newsmagazine year-in-review names winners only, no 2nd or 3rd, so only place 1 rows exist. It also says Jussi Oksanen (FIN) and Dresden Howell (USA) 'won big air titles' without saying which division each won; I left big air out rather than guess the divisions. Nationalities are given only for Vidal (France); the rest are null.
- World Championships of Snowboarding 2016 (Yabuli): the only results source found is TransWorld Snowboarding's report on what went wrong at the event. It gives slopestyle podiums for both genders, the women's big air podium, but only 1st and 2nd for men's big air - so no 3rd place row for that event. It also states the halfpipe event was cancelled, and an exhibition pipe ran later. Nationalities are recorded only where the article states them. Marked confidence 'likely'.
- World Snowboarding Championships 2012 (Oslo): halfpipe and slopestyle podiums are from contemporaneous reports (Snowboard Magazine for slopestyle, The Nordic Page for halfpipe). Neither prints scores, so score_or_time is null. The WSF's own 2012 highlights page names only the quarterpipe exhibition winner (Oliver Gittler) and gives no podium, and the WSF post announcing the halfpipe champions now 404s.
- FIS Junior World Championships older than 2021 (1997-2020) were not attempted - budget went to the full FIS senior set first. The route is proven and cheap: the WJC calendar page for a season gives the eventid(s), the event-details page gives every raceid, and each raceid page gives the podium.
