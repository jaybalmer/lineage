# Open questions from the v0.1 research wave

Every line is something a research agent looked for and could not source. This is both the wave-2 target list and the list worth asking the community.

## burton-opens (36)

- RESULTS ARCHIVE - Burton US Open: the organiser's site events.burton.com is frozen at the 2018 edition and its news archive lives at https://events.burton.com/burton-us-open/uso-latest/<article-slug>/ (e.g. https://events.burton.com/burton-us-open/uso-latest/jamie-anderson-yuki-kadono-win-2015-burton-us-open-slopestyle-titles/). Those article pages returned ROBOTS_DISALLOWED to this agent while https://events.burton.com/burton-us-open/history/ and /event-schedule/ fetched fine, so the block is intermittent rather than absolute - retry. No single machine-readable US Open results table was found anywhere; results appear to exist only as per-year news articles.
- RESULTS ARCHIVE - Burton US Open, secondary patterns worth harvesting in wave 2: https://www.snowboarder.com/news/<year>-us-open-<discipline>-results-and-photos (confirmed live for 2010 halfpipe and 2010 slopestyle); MountainZone's contemporaneous 2003 archive at https://www.mountainzone.com/2003/usopen/html/index.html (halfpipe), /usopen_slopestyle.html and /usopen_railjam.html - this is a full 2003 results microsite and may have siblings at mountainzone.com/<year>/usopen/; and worldsnowboardfederation.org, which carries TTR-era finals reports with full podiums and TTR points (e.g. https://www.worldsnowboardfederation.org/burton-us-open-report/ for the 2008 slopestyle).
- RESULTS ARCHIVE - LAAX Open: https://laaxopen.com/en/results/ holds only the last four years (2023-2026) and links FIS results PDFs; the full champion list 2016 onward is at https://laaxopen.com/en/hall-of-fame/ (winners only, no 2nd/3rd). Complete podiums for 2017 onward should come from FIS at fis-ski.com, since the event has been a FIS Snowboard World Cup stop from 2017 and a Freeski World Cup from 2023. Per-event result pages follow https://laaxopen.com/en/finals-<discipline>-<sport>-<gender>/ - unversioned slugs, so they are overwritten each year and are not an archive.
- RESULTS ARCHIVE - Burton European Open: no organiser archive survives. Best consolidated source found is the World Snowboard Federation news archive, searchable at https://www.worldsnowboardfederation.org/?s=Burton+European+Open , which carries finals reports with podiums for at least 2008, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2014 and 2015. Whitelines keeps an event index at https://whitelines.com/events/burton-european-open linking per-year results articles.
- RESULTS ARCHIVE - Burton Global Open Series stops (NZ, Australian, Canadian, Nippon): the World Snowboard Tour kept per-event pages at http://dev.worldsnowboardtour.com/events/<event-slug>-<year>/ (confirmed URL shape: burton-canadian-open-snowboarding-championships-2010). That host returned ROBOTS_DISALLOWED with a DNS failure on robots.txt here; worth retrying, and worldsnowboardtour.com / worldsnowboardfederation.org may mirror the same records.
- TOOL BLOCKER: the Wayback Machine was unreachable from this environment - web.archive.org returned SITE_BLOCKED to the fetcher and was not on the network egress allowlist for curl, so the CDX API could not be queried. opensnowboarding.com, the old US Open site, therefore could not be inspected at all. en.wikipedia.org was also unfetchable (cache-only to the fetcher, 403 through the proxy for the API). Re-running this wave with Wayback access should close most of the date gaps below, especially the Stratton-era US Open editions.
- TOOL BLOCKER: this session exhausted its 200-call web search budget partway through, so the later series (Burton Australian Open, Burton Canadian Open, Nippon Open, Rail Days, Backhill, Abominable Snowjam) were researched by following links from already-found pages rather than by direct search. Those series are the thinnest rows in this file and should be re-run first.
- US Open lineage, unresolved: Burton's own history page (events.burton.com/burton-us-open/history/) opens the timeline with the 1982 National Snowsurfing Championships at Suicide Six, organised by Paul Graves and won by Doug Bouton. The PSIA-AASI archive (thesnowpros.org/2024/09/from-the-archives-great-moments-in-snowboarding-history-part-2/) treats that as a separate event and says Jake Burton Carpenter organised the National Snowboarding Championships at Snow Valley in 1983, and that THAT event became the US Open. Recorded here as one series starting 1982, per the organiser, with the dispute flagged. Not resolved by this agent.
- US Open edition numbering is internally inconsistent across sources and needs a decision before edition_label is used as a display field. Counting from 1983: First Tracks calls 2002 the 20th year, AlpineZone calls 2009 the 27th, the Aspen Times reprint of Burton's history calls 2012 the 30th, and 2014/2015/2016/2017/2018/2019/2020 are billed as 32nd/33rd/34th/35th/36th/37th/38th - all consistent. But Snowboard Magazine called the 2010 edition the '29th Annual' and a MINI USA press release billed 2011 as the '30th Anniversary', both of which count from 1982. Ordinals were therefore recorded in edition_label only for the years where sources agree (2014 onward).
- US Open, was there a 2022+ revival? The Vail Daily reported in 2021 that the 2021 edition was cancelled, that Vail had not budgeted for 2022, and that Burton had confirmed the US Open would not return, pointing to a new Burton Mystery Series. No source was opened confirming any edition after 2020, so series.status is set to 'dormant' rather than 'defunct'. Needs a check of burton.com news 2022-2026 to close out.
- Burton Mystery Series: named by Burton as the replacement pinnacle event for the US Open, intended to be hosted in a different country each time. Not researched in this pass; it may warrant its own series row with a lineage note pointing at us-open-snowboarding.
- US Open dates 1982-2001 are entirely unsourced. Every edition from 1982 to 2001 has null start_date and end_date; the years and venues come from Burton's own history page and the Aspen Times reprint of it, which give no dates. Suggested sources for wave 2: Rutland Herald and Brattleboro Reformer archives (a Rutland Herald piece 'Vermont has 61 boarders at U.S. Open' was seen in search results but not opened), Stratton Mountain press archives, and Wayback captures of opensnowboarding.com and burton.com.
- US Open dates partially sourced and needing completion: 2003 (only the 2003-03-15 slopestyle report date), 2008 (slopestyle finals 2008-03-21, halfpipe finals the next day, start unknown), 2010 (halfpipe finals 2010-03-20 only). 2009 dates are the announced Global Open Series calendar dates, not a post-event confirmation.
- US Open 1982 venue detail: 'Suicide Six' is confirmed, but the city was left null. The ski area is in Pomfret / South Pomfret, Vermont near Woodstock and has since been renamed Saskadena Six; no source confirming the municipality was opened here.
- Burton European Open first year is disputed. Burton's own blog and Cooler's retrospective both say the BEO started in 1999 around Innsbruck, and Cooler called the January 2009 edition the tenth anniversary. But the World Snowboard Federation billed the January 2011 edition as the '12th Annual' and Snowboard Magazine billed the February 2012 edition as the '13th annual', both of which count 2000 as the first. The likeliest reconciliation is that the inaugural event ran in the 1999/2000 season and is referred to by either year. series.first_year is set to 1999 per the organiser; not resolved.
- Burton European Open missing editions: no source was opened confirming a 2000, 2007 or 2013 edition, so no rows were created for those years even though the event was almost certainly annual. 2002-2006 rows rest on a single retrospective (Cooler) that gives venues but no dates. Priority wave-2 fix: worldsnowboardfederation.org has BEO coverage back to at least 2008 and the TTR calendar archives should cover 2004 onward.
- Burton European Open at Avoriaz could NOT be confirmed. The venue list given by Onboard when the BEO ended names Seegrube, Fieberbrunn, Livigno and Laax only, and no other source opened here mentions Avoriaz. If an Avoriaz edition exists it is unsourced in this file.
- Burton European Open 2010 dates conflict: the TTR registration listing gives 2010-01-09 to 2010-01-16, the World Snowboard Federation event write-up gives 2010-01-10 to 2010-01-16. Kept the TTR dates with the conflict in notes.
- Burton European Open exact dates missing for 2014 and 2015 (only finals days sourced) and for every edition 1999-2006.
- LAAX Open exact dates are missing for 2017-2026. The Hall of Fame confirms the event was held each of those years but carries no dates; the results page only goes back four years. FIS calendars (fis-ski.com) should give exact competition dates for 2017 onward.
- LAAX Open 2018: the Hall of Fame lists a snowboard halfpipe champion but no snowboard slopestyle champion for 2018, so the slopestyle appears not to have been awarded. No source found saying why (weather cancellation is the obvious guess but was not confirmed). Needs checking before the 2018 edition row's disciplines are trusted.
- LAAX Open 2027 is scheduled for 2027-01-13 to 2027-01-16 per laaxopen.com and is billed as the 12th consecutive year. Not entered as an edition row because it has not been held.
- Burton Global Open Series start and end years are both weak. first_year is set to 2006 because the first series championship (Shaun White and Torah Bright, USD 100,000 each) was awarded at the March 2007 US Open, implying a 2006/07 inaugural season - but no source naming the first season was opened. last_year is set to 2011 because that is the last series championship confirmed (Kelly Clark at the 2011 US Open); the brief suggests the umbrella ran to roughly 2013 and the 2012 BEO still awarded Global Open Series points, so the end year is probably later than 2011.
- Burton Global Open Series season rosters: only the 2008/09 season is fully documented here (six stops, all dates). The 2009/10 and 2010/11 seasons are partially covered and 2006/07, 2007/08, 2011/12 and 2012/13 are not covered at all. Onboard Magazine has a 'Burton Global Open Series' article at onboardmag.com/news/burton-global-open-series.html that returned ROBOTS_DISALLOWED here and probably carries another season's full calendar.
- Burton New Zealand Open first year of 2003 is arithmetic, not a direct statement: the 2007 edition was reported as the fifth annual, the 2010 as the eighth annual and the 2011 as the ninth year, all of which back-date the first edition to 2003. No source naming a 2003 or 2004 edition was opened, so no rows were created for those years.
- Burton New Zealand Open end: no edition after 2011 was found, and no source was opened saying the event ended. Snow Park NZ, one of its two venues, closed permanently in 2011, which may be the reason - unverified. series.status left as 'unconfirmed'.
- Burton Australian Open is a near-empty row: only the 2008 edition (Perisher Blue, 2008-09-02 to 2008-09-06) is sourced, from the Global Open Series calendar, and even that gives no disciplines. First year, last year and every other edition are unknown. Perisher's own event archives and Australian snowboard media are the obvious next stop.
- Burton Canadian Open 2008/2009 ambiguity: the Global Open Series calendar announced 2008-12-01 to 2008-12-07 at Canada Olympic Park, but Onboard reported the Burton Canadian Open slopestyle results from the same venue on 2009-02-09. One edition row (2009) was created rather than two. Needs resolving - it changes whether the series ran in December or February and whether there is a missing edition.
- Burton Canadian Open first and last years are unknown; only 2009 and 2010 editions are sourced. No pre-2008 or post-2010 edition was found.
- Nippon Open naming and lineage: the event is recorded here as 'Nissan X-Trail Nippon Open', the name used in every source opened. Whether it was ever branded 'Burton Japan Open' or 'Burton Nippon Open', and whether an earlier Burton-branded Japanese Open preceded the Nissan title sponsorship, could not be confirmed - so former_names is null rather than guessed. Its first and last years are both unknown; only 2008 and 2009 editions are sourced.
- Burton Rail Days: only the fifth edition (2015-10-26, Roppongi Hills, Tokyo) is sourced, which back-dates the first edition to about 2011 but that was not confirmed and no other edition was found. Japanese-language sources and Burton Japan's site are needed.
- Burton Backhill: NOT ENTERED. The only lead found was snowboardcollector.com/backhill82, which returned ROBOTS_DISALLOWED (robots.txt DNS failure) on both the bare and www hostnames, so nothing about it could be verified - not the year, venue, format or whether it was a contest at all rather than a board model. Retry that page; the slug suggests 1982.
- Burton Abominable Snowjam: NOT ENTERED. No source about this event could be reached. The web search budget was exhausted before it was researched and every search-engine substitute tried (DuckDuckGo lite/html, Bing) was blocked, so no candidate URL was ever obtained. Entirely unresearched, not merely unverified.
- Other Burton-run contest series not covered in this pass and worth a look in wave 2: the Burton AM Series (a US amateur series - the TTR registration page lists Mount Hood, Bear Mountain, Winter Park, Northstar, Seven Springs and Waterville venues), Burton Qualifiers / Burton Open Qualifiers feeding the Opens, and the Burton Mystery Series.
- Discipline vocabulary friction: the 1995 US Open ran a women's super-G, which has no entry in the Linestry discipline list, and is recorded as 'other'. The 2009 US Open ran a best trick contest and a junior jam, also recorded as 'other'. Junior/junior-jam fields at the US Open, BEO and LAAX Open are divisions rather than disciplines and were not modelled - worth deciding before results are loaded, since juniors have their own podiums.
- Superpipe vs halfpipe: from 2000 the US Open pipe event was built as a superpipe but is called the halfpipe by every source, so all US Open pipe editions are recorded as 'halfpipe' with the superpipe transition noted on the 2000 edition. The series-level disciplines field lists both. Same question applies to the BEO and LAAX Open, which ride the LAAX superpipe.

## olympics-fis (12)

- ISF World Championships 1993-2002: only the 1993 edition (Ischgl, AUT) could be sourced, from Snowboarder magazine's 1990s history timeline (https://www.snowboarder.com/news/snowboard-history-timeline-part-31990s). English and German Wikipedia articles on the International Snowboard Federation confirm the federation held world championships and folded on 22 June 2002 but list no editions. Terje Haakonsen's Wikipedia article credits him with ISF halfpipe world titles in 1993, 1995 and 1997 without venues. NO ISF results archive was found anywhere online. Next step: TransWorld Snowboarding and Snowboarder print archives, and Wayback Machine captures of isf.ch / isf-world.com, which is the only plausible route to an ISF edition list and podiums.
- Motorola ISF World Championships 1999/2000: a Sportcal press release (https://www.sportcal.com/pressreleases/the-motorola-isf-world-championships-1999-2000-halfpipe-davos-sui/) describes an ISF halfpipe world championship final at Jakobshorn, Davos (SUI) on 8 April 2000, won by Ross Powers and Stine Brun Kjeldaas, with alpine finals the day before and boardercross to follow at Laax. It is unclear whether this was a single-venue world championship or the finals of a season-long 'Motorola ISF World Pro Tour' whose titles were decided on tour standings. Not entered as an edition row until that is resolved.
- FIS Snowboard World Cup season 2019/20: the FIS calendar view I read lists 33 event weekends and flagged no cancellations, but the season ran into the March 2020 COVID-19 shutdown. Cancellations have NOT been verified and no status_note is asserted. Next step: open each March 2020 row on https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/general/calendar-results.html?sectorcode=SB&seasoncode=2020&categorycode=WC and check for results.
- FIS Snowboard Junior World Championships 2020 (St. Lary FRA 23-25 Mar, Lachtal AUT 27 Mar - 1 Apr): both legs fall inside the COVID-19 shutdown. Recorded with status 'unconfirmed'. Next step: check for published results on the FIS per-race pages for those events.
- FIS Snowboard Junior World Championships 2023: the FIS calendar under seasoncode 2023 lists Cardrona (NZL) 28 Aug - 8 Sep 2023 alongside Bansko (BUL) and Passo San Pellegrino (ITA) in March 2023. It is unresolved whether the Cardrona leg belongs to the 2023 edition or is the opening leg of the 2024 edition; the 2018 Cardrona edition shows FIS uses the same season-coding quirk for southern-hemisphere legs. The 2024 edition row here excludes Cardrona.
- FIS World Cup event counts: the per-season counts recorded in edition notes are counts of FIS calendar rows, each of which can carry several individual races, and they include any events later cancelled. They have not been reconciled against races actually run and should be treated as indicative, not as a race count.
- Date disagreements between FIS and English Wikipedia for the FIS World Championships in 1999, 2001, 2005, 2009, 2013, 2019, 2023 and 2025 are recorded in each edition's notes. FIS calendar dates were preferred throughout. A later pass could pin exact competition days from the per-race pages.
- FIS World Championships 2027 (Montafon, AUT) and 2029 (Zhangjiakou, CHN) appear as future awards on the English Wikipedia article. No edition rows were created because they have not been held.
- Winter Youth Olympic Games 2012 (Innsbruck) and 2016 (Lillehammer): Olympedia edition ids for these two Games were not located, so venue and date detail comes from English Wikipedia rather than Olympedia. Verify against https://www.olympedia.org and olympics.com before promoting these two rows to 'verified'.
- Olympic edition rows carry Games-level snowboarding date ranges and a discipline list, but not the per-event competition days. Olympedia's per-Games index (https://www.olympedia.org/editions/{id}/sports/SBD) has exact dates for every individual event if that granularity is wanted later.
- The 2018 PyeongChang women's big air dates read from Olympedia (19-22 February) look like a qualification-to-final span rather than consecutive competition days; the edition row uses the Games-level snowboarding window 10-24 February instead.
- No results-archive URL was found for the ISF. For everything else in this wave the archives are: Olympedia https://www.olympedia.org/editions/{edition_id}/sports/SBD for Olympic and Youth Olympic snowboarding; FIS https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/general/calendar-results.html?sectorcode=SB&seasoncode=YYYY&categorycode=WC|WSC|WJC for World Cup, World Championships and Junior World Championships calendars; FIS https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/general/results.html?sectorcode=SB&raceid=NNNNN for individual race finishing orders; FIS https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/general/cup-standings.html?sectorcode=SB&seasoncode=YYYY&cupcode=WC&disciplinecode=ALL&gendercode=M|L for season Crystal Globe standings. All four patterns were opened and confirmed working.
